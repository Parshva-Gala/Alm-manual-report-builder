# The ESG materials, explained simply

The library answers four different questions: **what the bank must do; how ESG risks are assessed; how emissions are measured; and how these ideas have already been brought together.** Its value comes from combining those answers while keeping their purposes clear.

A regulation, a calculation standard, a scenario and a presentation are different kinds of evidence. Their importance depends on the question being asked. Source numbers below match the complete register in the [Banking ESG Framework](<C:/Users/Admin/ESG/ESG_Banking_Framework.md>), which contains the original file links and detailed references.

## Group 1 — Rules, disclosure guidance and green taxonomies

These materials explain responsibilities: who oversees ESG, how risks enter banking decisions, what must be reported, and when financing can be called green. They establish the applicable expectations. Requirements depend on the bank's country, entity and reporting circumstances; foreign rules can inform good practice without becoming local obligations.

**S01 — CBE circular and ESRMS manual, English translation.** Explains environmental and social checks throughout lending, from screening to monitoring and corrective action. Particularly useful for building a complete financing process. The supplied translation still needs reconciliation with the official Arabic.

**S02 — CBJ climate-risk instructions, English.** Explains how Jordanian banks should govern climate risk and connect it to strategy, credit, capital and information. It establishes responsibilities rather than supplying one universal climate-loss formula.

**S03 — Signed Arabic CBJ climate-risk instructions.** The Arabic counterpart to S02. Important when interpreting the original wording and authority. It represents the same instruction, so the two versions should not be treated as separate requirements.

**S04 — CBJ climate disclosure and reporting instructions.** Explains public climate disclosures and information submitted to the supervisor. Useful for identifying scope, reporting responsibilities and different reporting cycles. Public disclosure and supervisory returns have different purposes.

**S05 — CBJ Annex 2 reporting workbook.** Shows the actual information the supervisor expects: sector exposures, locations, provisions, maturities, emissions and green finance. Important for understanding precise amounts and reporting units; it is not an ESG scorecard.

**S06 — ASE climate disclosure guidance, English.** Explains how listed companies organise climate reporting around governance, strategy, risk management and metrics. Helpful for preparing a coherent report. Current operative rules take precedence over conflicting dates in this guide.

**S07 — ASE climate disclosure guidance, Arabic.** Provides the local-language counterpart to S06, with different page numbering. Useful for interpretation and communication. It adds no separate reporting obligation merely because it is another language version.

**S08 — ASE disclosure policy.** Explains the policy purpose, adoption approach and institutional responsibilities behind climate reporting. Useful background for understanding why the requirements exist. Use current operative rules for the precise reporting calendar.

**S09 — “Cliamte Disclosure Guidance” file.** This misspelled file is empty. It is retained in the inventory for completeness, but provides no evidence. The substantive English guidance is S06; an empty file cannot be assumed to contain it.

**S10 — Jordan National Green Taxonomy, English.** Defines which activities qualify under Jordan's green and specified transition categories. Important for defensible green-financing claims. Technical performance, environmental safeguards and social safeguards must be considered together; classification does not establish creditworthiness.

**S11 — Jordan National Green Taxonomy, Arabic.** The Arabic counterpart to S10. Useful for local interpretation of activity criteria. Both versions belong to the same taxonomy; they should be linked, with differences checked rather than counted as additional requirements.

**S12 — UAE climate-risk management principles.** Explains governance, information, risk assessment, capital, liquidity and scenario analysis. Useful as a supervisory management reference. The responsible UAE regulator determines its application to particular institutions.

**S13 — ADX ESG disclosure guidance.** Broadens the discussion beyond climate to employees, safety, diversity, communities, ethics, governance and privacy. Useful for the bank's own ESG performance and reporting. The guide is voluntary; separate applicable obligations remain relevant.

## Group 2 — Climate methods, scenarios and ESG ratings

These explain how to investigate ESG questions and translate climate changes into consequences. The Indonesian books provide the most complete connected climate methodology in the library. Other sources contribute future scenarios, banking-risk interpretation and examples. Their assumptions need interpretation: a historical dataset, hypothetical scenario or rating is not automatically a financial loss.

**S14 — OJK Book 1: General Guide.** Explains the bank-wide climate framework: governance, strategy, risk identification, oversight and reporting. It is the starting point for understanding how the six Indonesian books fit together, within their original country context.

**S15 — OJK Book 2: Technical Guide.** Explains how climate scenarios affect borrower finances, credit losses, market values, liquidity and bank operations. This is the main financial-transmission reference. Example coefficients and a few inconsistent passages require careful interpretation.

**S16 — OJK Book 3: Carbon Calculation Methodology.** Shows how activity information becomes an emissions estimate and provides example factors. Useful for practical data collection. Factors must fit the location, period and activity; the guide does not replace emissions-accounting standards.

**S17 — OJK Book 4: Macroeconomic Data.** Provides Indonesian scenario paths for economic and energy variables. Useful for understanding the wider assumptions behind a scenario. These are dated, country-specific values, not ready-made forecasts for another bank's market.

**S18 — OJK Book 5: Disaster Data.** Provides historical Indonesian flood/fire events and risk indices. Useful for location screening and concentrations. These records do not directly tell us a particular building's future damage or expected financial loss.

**S19 — OJK Book 6: Reporting Template.** Brings readiness, exposures, assumptions, financial effects and management responses into one reporting structure. Useful for explaining results consistently. Its original submission instructions belong to the historical Indonesian exercise.

**S20 — NGFS Phase V scenarios.** Describes alternative climate-policy and economic futures for resilience analysis. Useful for exploring different pathways, not predicting one certain outcome. Some supplied physical-loss estimates need qualification following the underlying research paper's retraction.

**S21 — BIS climate-risk FAQs.** Explains how climate risks enter existing banking frameworks such as credit, market, operational and liquidity risk. Important for connecting ESG to established banking disciplines. It does not introduce a universal ESG capital surcharge.

**S22 — EBRD climate-risk management note.** Gives a concise cycle: identify, assess, manage and report. Useful for deciding where simple screening is sufficient and where deeper analysis is needed. Its particular scoring and review practices are examples.

**S23 — Moody's 2023 climate assessment.** Shows how one organisation assesses locations, hazards, transition costs, suppliers and governance. Useful as a worked reporting example. Its company-specific results and proprietary credit analysis are not a general bank calculation method.

**S24 — MSCI ESG ratings methodology.** Explains selecting relevant issues, assessing exposure and management, and comparing companies with industry peers. Important for understanding what an ESG rating means. It is neither a default probability nor a green-activity certificate.

**S25 — Climate-related vulnerabilities file.** This file is empty. It cannot contribute a vulnerability method or support any conclusion. Keep the missing-material record visible so it can be replaced if the underlying publication is needed.

## Group 3 — GHG Protocol and PCAF emissions accounting

These establish what is counted and how emissions are measured. GHG Protocol defines company inventories and value-chain boundaries. PCAF explains the emissions associated with financial activities. This group matters because emissions figures are comparable only when their boundaries and methods are clear. Emissions measure a footprint; financial losses require a separate economic analysis.

**S26 — GHG Protocol Corporate Standard.** Establishes the organisation's emissions boundary, direct emissions, purchased energy, calculation principles and reporting controls. It is the inventory foundation. Read the older supplied edition with its later amendments and supporting guidance.

**S27 — Scope 2 Guidance.** Explains purchased electricity, heat, steam and cooling, including grid-based and contractual reporting. Important for renewable-energy claims. The two reporting views must be distinguished; adding them would count the same consumed energy twice.

**S28 — Corporate Value Chain Scope 3 Standard.** Defines the fifteen categories of value-chain emissions, exclusions and reporting requirements. Important because a bank's footprint extends beyond buildings and electricity. Financed emissions are only part of this wider review.

**S29 — Scope 3 Calculation Guidance, GHG-folder copy.** Provides practical calculation choices for each category, using supplier, activity or estimated data. Useful for turning the Scope 3 Standard into calculations. It supplements the standard rather than replacing its requirements.

**S30 — Scope 3 Calculation Guidance, shared-folder copy.** An exact duplicate of S29. It adds no new method or independent evidence. Keep both locations accounted for, but use one consistent interpretation of the guidance.

**S31 — PCAF Part A full standard.** Explains emissions attributed to loans and investments, with different methods for companies, projects, property, vehicles and sovereigns. Essential for financed emissions. The supplied 2022 edition must be distinguished from later editions.

**S32 — PCAF Part A executive summary.** Gives a short introduction to financed emissions and the main asset classes. Useful for orientation and explaining the approach to management. Detailed treatment and exceptions come from the full standard.

**S33 — PCAF Part B full standard.** Explains emissions associated with arranging relevant capital-market transactions. Important for banks that facilitate financing without simply holding loans or investments. Facilitated emissions have their own timing and must remain separate from financed emissions.

**S34 — PCAF Part B executive summary.** Summarises facilitation, attribution and transaction-year treatment. Useful for quickly understanding Part B's purpose. The full standard and scope appendix are needed to resolve detailed coverage and reporting questions.

**S35 — PCAF Part B scope appendix.** Clarifies which transactions and arranger roles fall within the method. Important because arranging, lending and ongoing administration are different activities. Some syndicated-loan wording needs a documented interpretation rather than an assumed calculation.

**S36 — PCAF Part C executive summary.** Introduces emissions associated with insurance underwriting. Useful for understanding a financial group's insurance activities separately from investments. Only the summary was supplied; it cannot establish every calculation and reporting rule.

## Group 4 — Existing explanations and historical applications

These show how the organisation has already tried to connect ESG concepts. They contribute practical examples, questions and banking structures. Their importance is continuity and usability: much useful thinking already exists. They remain examples, however; a presentation, questionnaire or old formula needs support from the relevant source before becoming a decision rule.

**S37 — Integrated ESG Guide for Egypt.** Brings the regulatory process, emissions, taxonomy, financial effects and reporting together in one explanation. Useful for understanding their relationships. It is an internal synthesis that depends on the underlying primary references.

**S38 — Integrated ESG presentation.** Explains the separate customer perspectives and how assessment, emissions, classification, risks and actions connect. Useful for communicating the overall idea. Its descriptions illustrate intended capability rather than independently validating methods.

**S39 — Refined ESG presentation.** Develops sector relevance, climate/credit links, portfolio views and monitoring. Useful for identifying the questions financial analysis must answer. Suggested score-to-credit or capital relationships require their own evidence and calibration.

**S40 — ESG presentation with screenshots.** Illustrates how customer, portfolio and action information can be presented. Useful for communicating the intended experience. Screenshots cannot establish that the underlying calculations, controls or data are correct.

**S41 — Integrated single-customer workbook.** Follows one customer through emissions, environmental/social checks, taxonomy and reporting. Useful for seeing several methods together. Its illustrative decisions should not become general lending rules, particularly requiring every loan to be green.

**S42 — GHG model-mapping workbook.** Lists activities, data fields and example emissions factors. Useful as a collection checklist. It is not a complete verified emissions engine; factors, units and inventory boundaries still need to be established.

**S43 — Green taxonomy questionnaire and scorecard.** Converts selected taxonomy activities and safeguards into assessment questions. Useful for structured evidence collection. It covers only part of the taxonomy, and questionnaire completion is not proof that an activity qualifies.

**S44 — IFRS S1/S2 financial-impact risk register.** Lists sustainability issues and possible effects on revenue, expenses, assets and liabilities. Useful for identifying financial connections beyond climate. These are examples, not confirmed customer findings or authoritative accounting conclusions.

**S45 — Historical Jordan ICAAP workbook.** Shows capital resources, risk assessments, stress results, planning and headroom. Useful for connecting ESG financial effects to capital adequacy. Its historical thresholds and score-based capital adjustments are not automatically valid current requirements.

The practical reading sequence is: establish applicable responsibilities from Group 1; understand the assessment methods in Group 2; use Group 3 for emissions accounting; then use Group 4 to make those ideas concrete. For any customer, keep its E&S acceptability, emissions, taxonomy result, financial risk and required actions separately visible.
